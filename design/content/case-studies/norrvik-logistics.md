Norrvik Logistics stores and ships goods for retailers and manufacturers from six distribution centres in Sweden, the largest in Jönköping. The centres run around the clock, and in 2023 Norrvik began moving its delivery fleet to electric vans and trucks. It asked us two questions: how much of its electricity could its own roofs give, and how could it charge a growing fleet without waiting years for larger grid connections?

## The problem

Before the first electric van arrived, the six centres used about 15 GWh of electricity a year, for lighting, conveyors, sorting, heating and the chilled zones. The plan for the fleet would add more than 3 GWh. Two of the centres were already close to the limits of their connections, and a larger connection at one of them had been quoted at more than two years. Meanwhile the roofs, about 60,000 m² in all, did nothing.

## What we built

Between April 2024 and June 2025, centre by centre:

- 9.4 MWp of ballasted east–west solar, 21,860 panels, on the six roofs
- 78 string inverters, mounted on the walls of the centres' plant rooms
- 184 charge points of 11 kW for vans, and 28 of up to 150 kW for trucks
- Ampfield Pilot at every centre, sharing each connection between the building and its chargers

## Building around centres that never close

A distribution centre has no quiet season, and Norrvik's six never close. We planned the work with each centre's manager so that no dock closed and no forklift route was blocked in working hours.

- Material went up onto the roofs by crane on Sunday mornings, when yard traffic is lowest, and was stored up there in marked areas.
- The system rests on ballast, so nothing was drilled through roofs above stored goods.
- Cable trenches for the chargers were dug in sections, each closed for at most three nights and covered with steel plates by day.
- Each centre was connected to its new supply in one planned stop of under four hours, early on a Sunday morning.

The survey found one surprise. At Borås, the older half of the roof had been re-roofed in 2012 with thicker insulation, and the extra weight had used up most of its margin. We moved those panels to the extension built in 2016. It cost a week of design and nothing on site.

## What it gave

In its first full year, July 2025 to June 2026, the solar gave 8.3 GWh. Norrvik used 7.1 GWh of it in its own centres and yards and sold the rest to the grid, mostly on summer weekends. That is 38% of the 18.7 GWh the six centres and their fleet used in the year.

The fleet took 3.2 GWh from its chargers in the same year, and no centre needed a larger connection for it. At Jönköping the 64 charge points could draw about 2.4 MW if every vehicle plugged in at once. Pilot shares out what the centre leaves spare, and the site has never gone over its limit. Pilot's monthly report now goes straight into Norrvik's sustainability report, centre by centre.
