Westerport Retail Parks owns and manages eleven retail parks between Teesside and South Yorkshire, with 168 tenants from supermarkets to garden centres. As landlord, it pays for the power in the car parks, the common areas and the service yards, and its tenants ask more each year what it is doing about the emissions from that power and their own.

## The problem

James Holroyd, Westerport's Head of Sustainability, had two aims with one deadline: cut the emissions of the landlord supply, and give visitors somewhere to charge, at all eleven parks by the end of 2026. The roofs belonged to Westerport, but nearly every one was over a tenant's shop. And each park's connection had been sized for lights and tills, not for fast chargers.

## What we built

From February 2025 to September 2026, park by park:

- 6.8 MWp of solar on 74 roofs, about 15,800 panels
- 280 charge points of 22 kW and 60 fast chargers of up to 150 kW, in the eleven car parks
- Ampfield Pilot at every park, sharing the connection between the landlord supply, the chargers and the solar, and selling the surplus to tenants at the four parks where they buy it

Westerport paid for the systems itself, as part of its investment in the parks. Our Leeds office maintains them under a ten-year service agreement.

## Working around open shops

Retail parks trade seven days a week, and Westerport's condition was that no tenant would lose a day's trading to the works.

- Every tenant was visited before work began on its roof, told which days the crew would be above it, and given the site manager's number.
- Roof work ran in the daytime behind exclusion zones agreed with each tenant, and cranes lifted before opening on Sunday mornings.
- Trenches for the chargers' cables were dug at night, a section of car park at a time, and filled before the first customers arrived.
- Each park's switch-over to its new supply took under four hours, at night.

At the largest park, a 1.1 MWp roof over a supermarket went up in eleven working days, with the shop trading below the whole time. Over nineteen months no park lost a trading day.

## What it gave

Eight of the parks ran through the summer of 2026 with their systems complete. On working days from April to September their roofs covered the whole landlord supply, the chargers included, from about eight in the morning to five in the afternoon. Over those six months they gave 3.1 GWh.

Drivers charged 1.2 GWh at the parks' charge points in the first eight months of 2026, and the fast chargers by the parks' cafés are the busiest. At three parks the connection now limits how many more chargers can be added, and Westerport has asked us to study batteries there.

Pilot reports each park's production, use and export every month, so Westerport's landlord emissions are now measured park by park, and tenants can be given their share.

> We report our landlord emissions park by park, and electricity used to be the line we could only buy our way out of. Now most of the daytime figure comes from our own roofs, and it is measured, not estimated.
>
> <cite>James Holroyd, Head of Sustainability, Westerport Retail Parks</cite>
