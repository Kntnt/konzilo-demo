Our Leeds team runs the systems we have built across the north of England: rooftop solar, batteries and chargers on factories, depots and retail parks, among them the 6.8 MWp and 340 charge points at Westerport Retail Parks' eleven parks. As more of what we build goes into service, we need another field service technician to keep it running.

## What the work is

- Planned maintenance of inverters, batteries and chargers, to a schedule set by our operations centre
- Finding and fixing faults, with the data from Ampfield Pilot in front of you before you arrive
- Thermal imaging of panels and connections, and inspections of roofs after storms
- Being on call one week in five
- Writing up each visit, so that the next technician knows what you found

Most of the sites are within two hours of Leeds, and most days start from home. You report to the UK service lead in Leeds, part of Elias Forsberg's Energy Services team, and work with the operations centre in Gothenburg, which sees every site's alarms.

## What you bring

- A recognised electrical qualification, and up-to-date knowledge of the wiring regulations
- Experience of fault-finding on inverters, chargers or other power electronics
- Training for work at height, or the willingness to take it with us
- A full driving licence
- Clear written English: your report is what the customer reads

## What we offer

- A permanent, full-time position, employed by Ampfield, not through an agency
- A salary of £38,000 to £44,000, depending on experience, with on-call pay and overtime on top
- An equipped electric van, a phone, a laptop, and the tools and protective equipment for the work
- 25 days of holiday plus bank holidays, and a pension
- Paid training on every product we install, on the makers' own courses

## How to apply

Send your CV and a few lines on why this job to [careers@ampfield.example](mailto:careers@ampfield.example) by 30 November 2027, with Leeds in the subject line. We read applications as they come in and may fill the position before then. No cover letter is needed. If we go on, the next steps are a thirty-minute call with the UK service lead and then half a day in Leeds, part of it at one of the systems we run, and you get an answer within a week of that meeting.
