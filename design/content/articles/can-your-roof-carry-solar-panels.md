A solar system on a flat roof adds 12 to 20 kg per m², about the weight of five centimetres of wet snow. Most roofs built for a northern winter have room for that. Some do not, and the drawings seldom say which, because a roof's margin is spent on what has been added to it since it was designed. In 2025 we surveyed 214 roofs in Sweden, Denmark, the Netherlands and the United Kingdom.[^1]

## What the panels weigh

The weight depends mostly on how the system is held down. On a membrane roof we prefer not to drill: the system rests on the roof and is held against the wind by concrete ballast, more of it near the edges and corners, where the wind lifts hardest.[^2]

| Mounting | Added load | Holes in the membrane | Suits |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Ballasted, east–west at 10° | 12–20 kg per m² | None | Most membrane roofs |
| Ballasted, south-facing at 15° | 18–30 kg per m² | None | Low buildings in sheltered places |
| Fixed to the deck, east–west | 9–13 kg per m² | Several hundred, each sealed | Roofs with little spare capacity |
| Clamped to metal sheeting | 9–12 kg per m² | None, the clamps grip the seams | Standing-seam and profiled sheet roofs |

East–west rows lean against each other, catch less wind and need less ballast, and they spread the day's output over the morning and the afternoon. A south-facing row gives about a tenth more per panel, but fewer panels fit, and each needs more weight.

## What the survey checks

A site assessment is half a day on the roof and in the switch room, and a report within two weeks. It answers four questions.

### Can the structure carry it?

Where the margin is unclear, a structural engineer works out what the roof can carry beyond its own weight and the design snow load. The answer is often less than the drawings suggest, because the margin is eaten over the years by:

- sprinkler mains, ducts and cable trays hung from the roof structure
- plant added after the building was designed, such as cooling units
- a thicker layer of insulation laid at re-roofing
- snow drifting against parapets, roof lights and taller buildings next door

Panels change the snow load too, as snow drifts between the rows, and the engineer checks that as well.

### Will the membrane outlast the panels?

Panels stay on a roof for 30 years. A membrane that must be replaced in ten means lifting the whole array: on a 1 MWp roof, some 2,300 panels and around 50 tonnes of ballast. We take core samples, check the membrane's age and type against its expected life,[^3] and look for wet insulation. If it has less than ten years left, we advise replacing it first, as part of the same job.

### Where can panels not go?

Insurers and fire services want an array divided into blocks with walkways between them, kept clear of fire walls, smoke vents and roof lights, and a way to cut the DC circuits that the fire service can reach from the ground. On most roofs this takes 10 to 20% off the area that looked usable from the air.

### Can the switchboard take it?

An electrical engineer checks where the inverters will connect to the main switchboard, whether the board has room, and how much power the site could send to the grid on a sunny Sunday, when the roof gives most and the building uses least.

## How a survey runs

1. We ask for the drawings, the structural calculations, the roof's history and 12 months of meter data.
2. On the visit we fly the roof with a drone, walk it, take core samples and look at the structure from below.
3. A structural engineer checks the roof where its margin is unclear.
4. We draw the layout: panels, walkways, fire breaks and cable routes.
5. You get a report with one of three answers, the systems that fit, and what any work on the roof costs.

## Have these ready for the survey

Without them, add the time it takes to measure the structure on site.

- The building's drawings and structural calculations
- The roof's age, type and warranty, and the date of any re-roofing
- The plant and services added to the roof since it was built
- The fire strategy drawing, with fire walls and smoke vents
- The single-line diagram of the main switchboard
- How to reach the roof: hatch, ladder and edge protection

:::

## What it costs to find out late

The assessment costs you nothing, and what it finds is cheap to act on before the design is fixed. Moving panels from a weak roof to a stronger one costs a week of drawing. Strengthening the structure under a finished array means scaffolding inside a working building, and a new membrane under it means lifting every panel.

> One of our six roofs had been re-roofed in 2012 with thicker insulation, and the extra weight had used up most of its margin. The survey found it, and the panels went on the newer extension instead. It cost us a week on paper. Finding it after installation would have cost us a roof.
>
> <cite>Karin Öberg, Head of Property, Norrvik Logistics</cite>

## The three answers

Of the 214 roofs we surveyed in 2025, 172 could take a ballasted east–west system as they were. Another 31 could take panels with changes: a lighter system fixed to the deck, or a smaller area that left out a weak part of the roof. The last 11 needed work first, nine a new membrane and two a stronger structure, and seven of them went ahead once it was done.

If you plan to re-roof in the next few years, have the roof surveyed first, so that the membrane and the panels can be one job. [Book a site assessment](/go/site-assessment/) and we will tell you what we need to see.
