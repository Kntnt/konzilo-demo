The eleventh park is a 1990s park on the edge of Doncaster, with nine units, a garden centre and 640 parking spaces. Its roofs now carry 0.62 MWp of panels and its car park 34 charge points. The panels went up in six weeks over the summer, and the park was switched over to its new supply on the night of 17 September. With it, the programme we began with Westerport in February 2025 is complete: 6.8 MWp of rooftop solar and 340 charge points for visitors, across eleven parks from Teesside to South Yorkshire.

## What the eleven parks have

- 6.8 MWp of solar on 74 roofs, about 15,800 panels
- 280 charge points of 22 kW and 60 fast chargers of up to 150 kW
- Ampfield Pilot at every park, sharing each connection between the landlord supply, the chargers and the solar

The panels feed the landlord supply: the car park lighting, the common areas, the service yards and the chargers. On working days from April to September the roofs cover all of it from about eight in the morning to five in the afternoon. What the landlord supply cannot use goes to tenants who buy it at four of the parks, and to the grid at the rest.

## Built around open shops

Westerport set one condition at the start: no tenant would lose a day's trading to the works. Roof work ran in the daytime behind exclusion zones agreed with each tenant, and the cranes lifted before opening on Sunday mornings. Trenches for the chargers' cables were dug at night, a section of car park at a time, and filled before the first customers arrived. Each park's switch-over to its new supply took under four hours, at night.

Most of the roofs are over tenants' shops, so every tenant was visited before work began on its roof, told which days the crew would be above it, and given the site manager's number. Two tenants asked us to move work away from their busiest weeks before Christmas, and we did. At the largest park, a 1.1 MWp roof over a supermarket, the panels went up in eleven working days, with the shop trading below the whole time.

Over nineteen months the condition held at every park.

> Nineteen months, eleven parks, and not one tenant had to close for a day. That was the condition we set at the start, and Ampfield kept it.
>
> <cite>James Holroyd, Head of Sustainability, Westerport Retail Parks</cite>

## What the first parks show

Eight of the parks have run through a summer with their systems complete. From April to August their roofs gave 2.8 GWh. Drivers charged 1.2 GWh at the parks' charge points in the first eight months of 2026, and the fast chargers by the parks' cafés are the busiest of all.

Westerport reports its landlord emissions park by park, and Pilot now gives it each park's own production, use and export every month, measured rather than estimated.

## What comes next

At three of the parks the connection now limits how many more chargers they can take, and the fast chargers there already wait for each other on Saturday afternoons. Westerport has asked us to look at batteries at those three, charged from the roofs in the morning and emptied into the chargers when the car parks fill. We will report on that when it is decided.

For our Leeds office, Westerport was the first programme in which we designed, built and now run a whole portfolio under one contract, and the team that built it now maintains it. To keep up, we are hiring field service technicians in Leeds; [Careers](/about/careers/) lists the open positions.
