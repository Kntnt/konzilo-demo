We have installed 95 MWh of battery storage on customers' sites since 2018, from the 1 MWh units beside depot chargers to the 5 MWh battery that keeps Kessel Packaging's plant in Dordrecht within its grid connection. None has caught fire. That record rests on decisions made at the design stage, and each of them can be explained to a fire officer or an insurer in an afternoon.

## What can go wrong

The risk in a lithium-ion battery is thermal runaway. A cell that overheats, from a fault inside it or from damage, breaks down and gives off heat and flammable gas. If the heat reaches the cells beside it, they follow. If the gas gathers in a closed space, it can ignite all at once.

## Choose the cells first

Every battery we have installed since 2021 uses lithium iron phosphate cells. They hold a little less energy per kilogram than the nickel-based cells in most cars, but they need a higher temperature to go into runaway, and they give off less heat when they do. On a site where a few tonnes more make no difference, that is an easy trade.

The battery's own management system watches the voltage and temperature of every cell and disconnects a module that drifts out of range. Ampfield Pilot passes its alarms to our operations centre in Gothenburg, which is staffed day and night.

## Contain one module

Ask the supplier for the large-scale fire test of the exact cabinet or container you are buying.[^1] In such a test one module is forced into runaway on purpose, and the report shows whether the fire spread to the next. A design that keeps a fire to one module is the basis of every other decision, and we buy no other kind.

## Deal with the gas

Every container we install has gas detection, ventilation that starts when the detectors find gas, and panels that give way under pressure and send the blast upwards, away from people. Inside are smoke and heat detectors and a suppression system.

A suppression system puts out flames, but it does not cool the cells, and a module that keeps heating can go on giving off gas. So the plan we agree with the fire service rests on cooling: keeping the containers beside a burning one cold with water from outside, and never opening its doors.

## Keep it away from what matters

Distance does more than any system. Our starting point for an outdoor battery is 6 metres to a building, a boundary or a route people use, or 3 metres behind a wall that holds back fire for 60 minutes. The fire service may ask for more, and its answer decides. At Brantevik Foods' plant in Tomelilla the battery stands 14 metres from the cold store, on its own concrete pad, with a fence 2 metres out on every side.

We avoid putting batteries inside buildings. It can be done, with a fire-rated room, its own ventilation and the insurer's written agreement, but a container outdoors costs less and is easier to defend.

## Write the plan with the fire service

Before a battery is switched on, the local fire service visits the site and receives the plan. Ours fits on two pages:

1. What the battery is: its chemistry, its energy, and where the containers stand
2. How to isolate it, and which isolators the fire service can operate itself
3. What not to do: open a container's doors while it is burning or giving off gas
4. How far to keep people back, and which way the wind takes the smoke
5. Whom to call: our operations centre, which can see every cell's temperature

A copy hangs in a box at the gate, and the plan becomes part of the site's own emergency plan, so that the people on shift know it as well as the fire service does.

## What your insurer will ask

- The cell chemistry, and the fire test report for the product
- The distance to buildings, boundaries and other plant
- How gas and smoke are detected, and whom an alarm reaches
- The emergency plan, and whether the fire service has seen it
- Who maintains the battery, and how often

We send these answers to the insurer with the design. In our experience an insurer who has them before the battery arrives asks for fewer changes after it. For the rest of what a battery needs, from the foundation to the connection, see [battery storage](/solutions/battery-storage/).
