This spring many of our customers report a year in which part of their fleet ran on electricity for the first time, and the question they bring us is how to count it. The diesel the vans no longer burn leaves Scope 1. The electricity they take enters Scope 2. In all four of our markets the second is smaller than the first, but by how much depends on choices an auditor will ask you to explain.

## Where the emissions move

Under the greenhouse gas accounting standard that almost every company reports by, fuel burned in vehicles you own or operate is Scope 1. Electricity you buy is Scope 2, whether it runs a conveyor or charges a van. Leased vans usually count as your own if you run them day to day. Move a van from diesel to electricity, and its emissions move from the first line to the second.

## Sixty vans, four countries

Take a fleet of 60 large vans, each driving 150 km on 250 working days: 2.25 million km a year. On diesel, at 9 litres per 100 km, they burn about 202,500 litres, which is about 530 tonnes of CO<sub>2</sub>e in Scope 1.[^1]

Electric, at 0.25 kWh per km measured at the charger, the same fleet takes about 560 MWh a year.[^2] With illustrative grid factors for each country, the location-based Scope 2 figure is:

| Country | Grid factor, kg CO<sub>2</sub>e per kWh | Scope 2, location-based | Cut against diesel |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Sweden | 0.03 | 17 tonnes | 97% |
| Denmark | 0.12 | 67 tonnes | 87% |
| United Kingdom | 0.20 | 112 tonnes | 79% |
| Netherlands | 0.30 | 168 tonnes | 68% |

The fleet's emissions fall in every country, and most where the grid is cleanest.

## Under the market-based method, the contract decides

The standard asks for Scope 2 in two ways, and the second, market-based, can give a very different answer. It counts the power at the emissions of what your contracts say you bought. If the depot's supply is backed by certificates of renewable origin, cancelled for you, the vans' electricity counts at close to zero. If it is not, it counts at the residual mix, the power left once every certified megawatt-hour has been claimed, which carries more carbon than the grid average in all four markets. In Sweden it carries several times more. A Swedish fleet that reports 17 tonnes location-based can report well over 100 tonnes market-based.

Power from your own roof that goes straight into the vans is neither bought nor burned, so it adds nothing to either figure.

## Where the vans charge

Where a van charges does not change which scope its power belongs to. It changes how good your figures are.

- **At the depot**, the chargers' own meters measure every session, and if drivers start with a card, every vehicle. This is the best data you will have.
- **At public chargers**, the energy is on the charging operator's invoice or the fuel card statement, in kWh. It is still electricity you bought. Unless the operator can show certificates for it, count it at the residual mix under the market-based method.
- **At drivers' homes**, where the company pays for the power, the energy is often estimated from distance. Count it, say that it is estimated, and say how.

Say in the report how much came from each. Auditors ask, and a fleet that charges 90% at the depot has better figures than one that charges half on the road.

## When the landlord pays the bill

If the depot's chargers are fed from a landlord's supply and the power is recharged to you, it is still your vehicles' electricity and still your Scope 2. The landlord should not report it as well. Agree in writing before the year starts which kilowatt-hours each of you reports, and on what meter.

## What to measure

1. Energy per vehicle from the depot chargers, by month
2. Energy per vehicle from public chargers, from invoices
3. Home charging, metered where the charger has a meter, otherwise estimated
4. Distance per vehicle, from the vehicles' own records
5. How much of the depot's energy came from your own roof, hour by hour
6. The supply contracts and certificates behind the power you bought

Ampfield Pilot gives the first and the fifth for every depot it runs, in the monthly report.

## What not to claim

- **"A zero-emission fleet."** Under the location-based method no grid is zero, and under the market-based method only certified or self-generated power counts as zero. Give the figures instead.
- **Exports as a credit.** Power your roof sends to the grid at noon does not cancel what the vans take at night. You may mention it separately, but not subtract it.
- **Avoided diesel as a cut in Scope 2.** Report the year's Scope 1 and Scope 2 as measured, and show the change against your base year.
- **Last year's factors.** Use the factors for the reporting year that your auditor accepts, and the same ones throughout the report.

If your vans charge on a site with a solar roof, count the roof's share hour by hour. The trucks at Norrvik's depot in Jönköping took 44% of their noon top-ups straight from the roof between April and September, and none in January. Set the roof's yearly output against the fleet's yearly use, and you claim solar power for nights and winter mornings when the roof gave none.

[^1]: About 2.6 kg CO<sub>2</sub>e for each litre of diesel burned. This factor and the grid factors in the table are rounded and given only to show the range; use the factors your reporting year and your auditor call for.

[^2]: The average over a year, measured at the charger at Norrvik Logistics' depot in Jönköping from October 2024 to September 2025. It includes charging losses and preconditioning, and ran from 0.23 kWh per km in June to 0.28 in January.
