A solar roof may not send a kilowatt-hour to the grid until the network operator has approved its connection. Until now we applied for that approval once the customer had signed and the design was finished. In 2025 that order cost some of our customers time and money. Of the 112 solar systems we designed, 19 had to be changed when the operator answered: 14 were given a limit on how much they could export, and 5 had to be made smaller. Each change added seven weeks on average, and two projects ran to a year from the first visit.

## What changes

From 2 March, every site assessment includes a capacity enquiry. It is a short request to the network operator, made in your name, that gives the site's connection, a year of its meter data and the largest system its roof could take, and asks what the site could export and whether the grid would need any work.

- **For you:** one signed page that lets us ask on your behalf. The enquiry costs nothing and commits you to nothing.
- **The report** still comes within two weeks of the visit, with the systems that fit and their prices. Operators answer in three to eight weeks. If the answer changes anything, you get a revised report before you decide.
- **For a site that plans chargers or a battery**, the same enquiry asks what more the site could draw, not only what it could send.

## Why the answer matters

A roof gives most on a sunny Sunday in June, when the building uses least. The cable and transformer that serve the site may already carry the export of other roofs nearby, and the operator has to be sure the voltage stays within limits on that Sunday. Its answer is one of four:

- **Approved as applied for.** Most of our applications in Sweden and Denmark.
- **Approved with an export limit.** The site may send no more than a set number of kilowatts. Ampfield Pilot holds the export below it by slowing the inverters when the building uses little.
- **Approved if the grid is reinforced**, at a cost the operator states and the site pays.
- **Approved with further protection**, such as a relay that disconnects the system if the grid's voltage or frequency strays.

An export limit sounds worse than it is. We size systems for use on site, so they export little. Of the 14 roofs given a limit last year, 11 kept their full size, and Pilot's limit costs each of them less than 1% of its year's output. Learning about the limit after signing was the costly part, because the design had to be done twice.

## How a grid application runs

1. **Capacity enquiry.** From March, at the site assessment.
2. **Application.** Once you have decided, we send the operator the single-line diagram, the inverters' certificates and settings, the protection, and the system's final size.
3. **The operator's study.** For larger systems, the operator works out what the export does to the voltage and to the local transformer. This takes from a few weeks to several months.
4. **Connection offer.** The operator's terms: the capacity, any limit, the cost of any work on its side, and a date.
5. **Work on the grid side.** Often only a new meter; sometimes a new cable or transformer, which sets the pace of the whole project.
6. **Commissioning.** We test the protection and the settings, and some operators send someone to watch.
7. **Permission to export.** The system is switched on for good.

The order is the same in all four countries. The time it takes differs: in Sweden and Denmark the operator's answer usually comes within weeks, while in congested parts of the Netherlands and England a study, a limit or a wait for reinforcement is more common.

## What it will not change

A typical 1 MWp roof still takes six to nine months from the first visit to the first kilowatt-hour, and the operator's approval is still much of that time. The enquiry does not make the operator faster. It moves the answer to the start of the project, when a limit changes a sketch rather than a finished design, and it should stop the projects that ran to a year.

If you have a report from us dated before March, it was made without the operator's answer. Ask your contact, and we will send the enquiry now.
