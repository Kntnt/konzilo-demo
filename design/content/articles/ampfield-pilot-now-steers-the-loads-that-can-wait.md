A cold store at −25 °C holds a great deal of cold. A tank of compressed air, a hot-water cylinder and a building's heating can all wait an hour without anyone noticing. Until now, Ampfield Pilot has used batteries, chargers and heat pumps to keep a site below its limits, and only watched the rest. From 3 November it can steer these loads too.

## What is new

- **Flexible loads.** You mark which loads may move, and how far: a cold store's set point by up to three degrees, an air compressor's tank between two pressures, a water heater by a few hours. Pilot runs them harder when power is cheap or the sun is up, and holds them back before a peak.
- **Limits by the hour.** A site whose connection is reduced at set hours can enter those hours, and Pilot plans the battery, the chargers and the flexible loads around them.
- **One order of priority.** You choose what gives way first when the site nears its limit: vehicles that leave late, the battery's own charging, then the loads you have marked.
- **The month's peak, explained.** The monthly report now shows what was running when each month's highest peak was set, and what that peak cost.

## Where it came from

Since 2025, Pilot has lowered the set point of Brantevik Foods' cold stores in Tomelilla when the roof gives more than the plant uses. The stores cool a few degrees further on solar power, and the compressors rest through the late afternoon while the stores drift back. The 4 MWh battery covers what is left of the peak. It worked well enough that other customers with cold stores, compressed air or large hot-water systems asked for the same.

The hourly limits came from the Netherlands, where network operators have begun to offer connections that give full capacity most of the year but less at hours they name in advance. Several of our Dutch customers have been offered such terms, and a site can only accept them if something plans around those hours.

## What it means for a site

At Brantevik in June, pre-cooling moved about a megawatt-hour of the compressors' work on each sunny day from the evening into the middle of the day, where the roof paid for it. In trials at five customers' sites since June, moving flexible loads lowered the month's highest peak by 6 to 11% before the battery did anything. At a site with a battery, that leaves more of the battery free to earn from the grid, or lets a smaller one do the same job. At a site without one, it can be enough to stay below a limit without buying one.

## What Pilot will not touch

Pilot moves only what you have marked as flexible, and only within the bounds you set. Production lines, lighting and everything else are measured but never switched. A cold store's sensors stay in charge: if any of them reads outside the range your quality team has set, Pilot stops moving that store and gives it back to its own control.

If the site would still pass its limit with every flexible load held back, Pilot raises an alarm to you and to our operations centre. It does not decide for you what to stop.

## When you get it

The update reaches every site that runs Pilot on 3 November, as Pilot's updates do every two weeks, with nothing to install and no stop. It changes nothing until you mark a load or enter a limit. Your contact in Energy Services will call before the end of the year to go through what your site could move, starting with sites that have cold stores or a connection with reduced hours.

The new functions are part of [Pilot's control level](/solutions/ampfield-pilot/) at no extra cost.
