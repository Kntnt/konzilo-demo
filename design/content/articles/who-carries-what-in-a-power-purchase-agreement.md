Lindhagen Furniture's factory in Värnamo gets 31% of its electricity from 2.2 MWp of panels on its roof, and Lindhagen did not pay for them. We own the system, insure it and maintain it, and Lindhagen buys every kilowatt-hour it produces at a price fixed for 15 years. About a third of the solar systems we built in 2025 were sold this way, and the rest were bought outright. Neither is better in general. They divide cost and risk differently.

## How the agreement works

We survey, design and build the system at our cost, and we own it for the term. You pay for the energy it produces, measured by a meter at the inverters, at a price per kilowatt-hour agreed before we build. You pay nothing for power the system does not produce. What your site cannot use flows to the grid, and your electricity supplier pays you the market price for it. We size the system so that at least 80% of its output is used on site.

We set the price from the system's cost, its expected yield over the term, and what the same kilowatt-hour costs you from the grid today, energy and grid fees together. The price starts below that, so the agreement saves money from the first month at today's prices. Whether it keeps saving depends on what grid power costs over the next 15 years, which nobody knows.

## Who carries which risk

| Risk | Bought outright | Power purchase agreement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paying for the system | You, before it runs | Ampfield |
| The roof gives less than forecast | You | Ampfield, since you pay only for what is produced |
| Faults and repairs | You, or a service agreement | Ampfield |
| New inverters around year 12 to 15 | You | Ampfield |
| Grid power gets cheaper | You save less | Your price may end up above the grid's |
| Grid power gets dearer | You save more | You save more |
| You sell the building or leave it | The system goes with the building | A buyer or new tenant takes over the agreement, or you end it early |

The one risk the agreement gives you that buying does not is the price. If grid power becomes much cheaper, you keep paying the agreed price for the solar share of your use. If it becomes dearer, the agreement looks better every year. Since 2021, grid power in our four markets has swung far more than any agreed solar price, and that is the main reason customers choose a known one.

## What it costs over the term

If you can pay for the system from cash you have no better use for, buying it costs less over its life. The price under an agreement covers our capital, insurance, maintenance and margin, so over 15 or 20 years you pay more in total than the system cost to build. The agreement makes sense when your capital earns more in your own business, when you would rather not own and run power plant, or when the decision to invest would take a year that the agreement does not.

Lindhagen had a new machining line to pay for in the same two years.

> Every krona we put into the roof would have been a krona less for the new line. The agreement meant we did not have to choose.
>
> <cite>Maria Lindhagen, Chief Executive, Lindhagen Furniture</cite>

## The clauses to read twice

1. **The end of the term.** What you may do when it runs out: extend at a lower price, buy the system at the price the agreement sets for that year, or have us remove it and restore the roof at our cost.
2. **Ending it early.** A table of what it costs to end the agreement in each year of the term, which is also the price at which you can buy the system.
3. **A new owner.** The agreement follows the building, so a buyer or a new tenant takes it over with the owner's consent. Check what happens if nobody does.
4. **Roof works.** Who pays to lift and refit the panels if the roof needs repair. Ours shares the cost when the work is planned, and we carry it when our system caused the damage.
5. **Long outages.** What we owe you if the system stands still for weeks, beyond the power you did not have to buy.
6. **The price itself.** Whether it stays fixed for the whole term or rises by a set percentage a year. We offer both, and a rising price starts lower.

## The accounts

Whether the agreement counts as a lease in your accounts depends on the accounting standards you report under and on its terms. Take a draft to your auditor before you sign, not after. The same goes for how the power is counted in your sustainability report, which we wrote about in [On-site solar in your sustainability report](/insights/on-site-solar-in-your-sustainability-report/).

Every offer we make shows both ways side by side: the system's price bought outright, and the price per kilowatt-hour under an agreement, each with its yield month by month. If the sums are close, choose the one whose risks you would rather hold.
