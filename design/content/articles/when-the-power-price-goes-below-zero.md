On the Sunday after Ascension Day this year, the day-ahead price of power in southern Sweden stayed below zero from ten in the morning until four in the afternoon. Across our Swedish roofs that day, Ampfield Pilot held back 41 MWh that would otherwise have gone to the grid. Their owners lost nothing by it. Had the power been exported, most of them would have paid to send it.

## Why the price goes below zero

In the hours when every roof in a region produces and few people use power, on a sunny weekend or a holiday, more power is offered than is wanted. Some producers keep running even when the price is negative: plants that cannot stop and start in an hour, and generators that are paid per kilowatt-hour whatever the market price. The price falls until enough of the others stop.

The hours have become common:[^1]

| Market | Hours below zero in 2025 | Of them, between 10:00 and 16:00 from April to August |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Southern Sweden | about 310 | 78% |
| Denmark | about 380 | 74% |
| Netherlands | about 520 | 81% |
| Great Britain | about 190 | 66% |

Most of them fall in the hours a solar roof produces most, and on the days a factory or warehouse uses least.

## What a negative hour costs you

Your contracts decide it.

- **Power you use yourself** still saves what you would have paid for it. Use on site is never the problem.
- **Power you export** is paid for under your export contract. Many pay the hourly market price less a fee, and in an hour at −€30 per MWh, each megawatt-hour you export costs you €30 and the fee. Some contracts pay nothing in negative hours instead, and a few pay a fixed price whatever the hour. Read yours.
- **Under a power purchase agreement** you pay us the agreed price for every kilowatt-hour the roof produces, exported or not. An export in a negative hour costs you twice: our price for the power, and the market's price for sending it away.

## What Pilot does

Each afternoon, when the next day's prices are published, Pilot looks for the hours in which exporting would cost money after the fees in the site's contract. For each such hour it sets the export limit to zero. The inverters slow until the roof gives only what the building uses, and a battery, if the site has one, charges from the roof first. Depot chargers that can take power then speed up.

Pilot has done this since the spring of 2024 for every roof whose inverters it controls. Under a power purchase agreement, the power the roof does not produce is power you do not pay us for, so we lose that sale. We hold the export back all the same, because the alternative is that you pay twice for something nobody wanted.

## What it costs in output

Less than most owners expect. Our roofs are sized for use on site, so they export little, and the negative hours fall on a few dozen days. Across all the roofs Pilot controlled in 2025, it held back about 2,700 MWh, 0.8% of what they produced, and saved their owners about €110,000 that they would otherwise have paid to export it.

The share differs a great deal between sites:

- **A roof sized to the building's use** loses 0.2 to 0.5% of its year.
- **A factory closed at weekends** loses more, 1 to 2%, because its surplus falls on Saturdays and Sundays.
- **A roof built larger than the building's use**, to fill the roof, can lose 4% or more, and the hours it loses are the hours it was built for.

That is the strongest reason we size roofs to the building's daytime use, since a surplus at noon can now cost money to export.

## What to check this summer

1. **Your export contract:** what it pays, or charges, in an hour with a negative price.
2. **Your supply contract:** a fixed price, or one that follows the hourly market.
3. **Your inverters:** whether they accept an export limit sent from outside. Most made since 2020 do.
4. **Who sets the limit:** a limit that nobody updates each day does nothing on the day it is needed.

If your roof was built by someone else and exports on sunny weekends, Pilot can read and control it. Send us last summer's meter data and your export contract, and we will show you how many of its hours cost you money.
