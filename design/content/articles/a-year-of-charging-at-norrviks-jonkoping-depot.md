The charge points at Norrvik Logistics' distribution centre in Jönköping went into service in September 2024, with the 3.2 MWp roof above them. From October 2024 to September 2025, the first full year, they charged 1.04 GWh into Norrvik's vans and trucks. Ampfield Pilot recorded every session, by vehicle and by charger, minute by minute.

## The depot

Jönköping has 52 charge points of 11 kW for vans and 12 chargers of up to 150 kW for trucks. If every vehicle plugged in at once they would draw about 2.4 MW, more than the centre's connection gives. Pilot shares out what the building leaves spare, slowing the chargers when the conveyors and the chilled zones need power and speeding them up when they do not.

The fleet grew through the year, from 38 vans and 6 trucks in October to 52 vans and 11 trucks in September. The vans deliver in and around Jönköping and stand in the yard from early evening. The trucks run regional routes, charge overnight and top up at the docks around noon while they are loaded.

## What the cold added

| At Jönköping | June 2025 | January 2025 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| A van's energy per km, measured at the charger | 0.23 kWh | 0.28 kWh |
| A van's energy per working day | 34 kWh | 41 kWh |
| Time the last van was full, on average | 01:40 | 04:10 |
| Share of the whole fleet's energy from the roof | 19% | 0% |

In January the vans took about a fifth more energy per kilometre than in June. Part of it heats the cabin and the battery on the road. Part is preconditioning: Norrvik's vans warm themselves on the charger for half an hour before they leave, so that the heat comes from the grid and not from the range. Charging itself loses a little more in the cold.

The nights were long enough all the same. On the coldest night, at −19 °C, the last van was full at 04:50, forty minutes before the first left. Three times in the year a van left below the charge its route needed, and each time it had been plugged in after three in the morning.

## The limit held

The highest demand the centre drew from the grid all year was 2.09 MW, on a Monday morning in January, when the trucks' morning charge overlapped the start of the day shift. That is 95% of what the connection allows. Pilot slowed the van chargers that had the longest time left until departure, and the peak passed in twenty minutes.

From April to September the noon top-ups of the trucks took 44% of their energy straight from the roof. Over the whole year, 11% of the energy the fleet took came from the panels above it. The vans charge at night, so most of the roof's power goes to the building.

## What broke

The chargers were available 99.2% of the year. Pilot logged 37 faults:

- 14 damaged cables or connectors, mostly run over in the yard or pulled from a moving van
- 9 chargers that lost contact with Pilot and kept charging at a safe low rate until it was back
- 8 faults in a charger's own electronics
- 6 faults on the vehicle's side, which we passed to Norrvik's fleet supplier

Our operations centre in Gothenburg cleared 21 of them remotely. For the rest a technician from Gothenburg was on site within a working day, and the average fault was cleared in 19 hours. After the first cables were run over, Norrvik moved the yard's markings and we fitted cable holders on the van chargers. In the second half of the year, two cables were damaged, against twelve in the first.

## Per vehicle, every month

Every charging session starts with the driver's card, so Pilot knows which vehicle took how much and when. Each month Norrvik's fleet manager gets the energy per vehicle and per kilometre, and its finance team gets the cost per vehicle at the price the centre paid in each hour. A van's energy costs Norrvik about a quarter of what its diesel did.

The same figures go into Norrvik's sustainability report, measured rather than estimated from distance, and the share that came from its own roof is counted apart. The report for 2025 will be the first in which Norrvik gives its fleet's electricity per vehicle.

> A year ago I could tell the board what the depot cost. Now I can tell them what each van costs per kilometre in each month, and why January costs more than June.
>
> <cite>Karin Öberg, Head of Property, Norrvik Logistics</cite>
