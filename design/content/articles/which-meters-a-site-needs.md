The meter the network operator fits at a site's connection measures what crosses it, in each direction, every quarter or half hour. Before a site has its own roof, that is almost all anyone needs. Afterwards the questions multiply. How much did the roof give, and how much of it did we use? What did the vans take, and what does the tenant in unit 4 owe? Each answer needs a meter of its own, in the right place, read at the same moments as the others.

## The five meters

| Meter | Where it goes | What it measures | Who uses the figure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Main meter | At the grid connection; the operator's | Import and export, each quarter or half hour | The supplier's and the network's bills, Scope 2 |
| Generation meter | On the inverters' output, before any load | Everything the roof produces | Billing under a power purchase agreement, use on site, yield checks |
| Battery meter | At the battery's connection | Energy in and out | The battery's losses and earnings |
| Charger meters | In each charger, or one per group | Energy per session and per vehicle | Fleet costs, billing staff, visitors or tenants, fleet emissions |
| Sub-meters | On each tenant's supply, or each large process | Use per tenant or per line | Recharging tenants, finding waste |

No figure a site reports comes from one meter alone. Use on site is generation less export. The building's own use is import plus generation, less export, less what went into the battery and the chargers. Each sum takes readings from several meters, so they must be read at the same instants. A generation meter whose clock runs fifteen minutes slow moves the roof's output into the wrong quarter-hour, and on a day of passing clouds the sums stop adding up.

## Billing grade or not

A meter that someone is billed from, a tenant, a visitor or a customer under a power purchase agreement, must be of a class approved for billing, and sealed. The display on a charger is not such a meter unless the charger is certified for it, and many are not. Meters that only help you understand your own site need not be approved, but should be accurate to within 1%.

A billing-grade sub-meter with its current transformers costs €400 to €900 installed. Fitting the four that most existing sites lack costs €3,000 to €6,000, less than a year of arguing over estimates.

## The mistakes we find

At our site assessments in 2025 we found a fault in the existing metering at about one site in fifteen. The common ones:

- **Current transformers fitted the wrong way round**, so that a meter counts export as import, or a load as negative.
- **A generation meter after a load**, on a board that also feeds the plant room, so that the roof seems to give less than it does.
- **A battery counted as load**, so that its charging appears as the building's use and its losses vanish.
- **Chargers on a general board without a meter of their own**, so that the fleet's energy has to be estimated from distance.
- **Monthly readings only**, which cannot show the hour a peak was set or the hour a roof's surplus went to the grid.
- **Clocks that drift**, on meters that are read on their own and never compared.

Each of these gives figures that look plausible for a year, until someone tries to reconcile them. The first to try is often the auditor.

## Who owns what

The main meter belongs to the network operator or the supplier, and its data reaches you through them, usually the next day. The generation meter is yours if you own the system, and ours under a power purchase agreement, which bills from it. Charger meters come with the chargers. The data from all of them is yours, whoever owns the meter.

## Before the electrician leaves

Ask for these on the day the meters go in, while the people who fitted them are still on site.

1. Each meter's direction checked against a load you know
2. Each meter's clock set to the same time, and how it is kept there
3. Each meter drawn on the single-line diagram, with its number
4. On a sunny hour, generation less export compared with what the building used
5. The meters that bill anyone, sealed, with their certificates

:::
