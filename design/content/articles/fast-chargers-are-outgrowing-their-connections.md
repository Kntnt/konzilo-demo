When Westerport Retail Parks opened its first fast chargers in 2025, the question was whether drivers would use them. Ten months into 2026 the question at three of its eleven parks is how to serve the drivers who queue for them on a Saturday afternoon, when the park's grid connection is already full.

## A sixth of the chargers, seven tenths of the energy

From January to October 2026, the charge points at Westerport's parks delivered 1.55 GWh, as Pilot recorded it from each charger's own meter, counting each charge point from the day it went into service:

| January to October 2026 | Chargers of 22 kW | Fast chargers of up to 150 kW |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Charge points | 280 | 60 |
| Energy delivered | 0.45 GWh | 1.10 GWh |
| Average session | 12 kWh | 27 kWh |
| In use, share of opening hours | 6% | 15% |
| In use on Saturday afternoons | 14% | 42% |

The fast chargers are 18% of the charge points and delivered 71% of the energy. The busiest of them stand by the parks' cafés, where a driver can eat while the car takes 30 kWh in twenty-five minutes. At the five parks that had fast chargers through both summers, the energy they delivered from June to August rose by 64% between 2025 and 2026.

## Where the connection runs out

Each park's grid connection was sized for car park lights, common areas and tills. Ampfield Pilot shares it between the landlord supply, the chargers and the roof's output, and when it is full, the chargers slow down. On a weekday that seldom shows. On a Saturday afternoon at the three busiest parks, the fast chargers are in use more than 80% of the time, Pilot holds them to about 70 kW each, half what they could give, and a session takes twice as long. Drivers wait for each other, and some leave.

A larger connection at those parks would take years in the north of England, and cost more than the chargers did. The other answers come sooner.

## Four ways to serve the queue

**A battery.** A battery of 1 MWh and 500 kW beside a park's fast chargers charges from the roof in the morning and from the grid at night, and discharges into the chargers when the car park fills. On a Saturday it adds 500 kW for two hours, enough to run six fast chargers at full power through the busiest part of the afternoon. It costs €380,000 to €450,000 installed. It pays back from the extra energy sold at fast-charging prices, and from the peaks it takes off the landlord supply on weekdays.

**A price by the hour.** Westerport sets its own charging prices. A higher price on Saturday afternoons, and a lower one on weekday mornings, moves the drivers who can choose and leaves the fast chargers to those in a hurry. It costs nothing to try, and Pilot can change the price by the hour.

**More slow chargers.** A visitor who stays two hours takes about 20 kWh on a 22 kW charger, more than most need to get home. A slow charger costs a fraction of a fast one and fits inside the connection when Pilot manages it. Signs that tell drivers so can shift a share of the queue.

**Flexible terms on the connection.** In parts of England, network operators offer more capacity on terms that let them reduce it at hours named in advance. A car park busiest on Saturday afternoons, when the wider grid is quiet, may suit such terms well. Ask what the reduced hours have been, not only what the contract allows.

> The fast chargers have done what we hoped: drivers come for them and stay for lunch. Now we have to keep them at full power on Saturday afternoons, when most people want them.
>
> <cite>James Holroyd, Head of Sustainability, Westerport Retail Parks</cite>

## What we are studying

At the three parks where the connection now limits the chargers, we are modelling all four for Westerport, from each park's minute-by-minute data since its chargers opened. The study looks at what each option would have added in kilowatt-hours delivered and drivers served on each Saturday of 2026, and what it costs. We expect to finish it in December, and will report what Westerport decides.

## For other car parks

Most retail and leisure car parks we see in Britain were connected for lights and tills, as Westerport's were. If yours has fast chargers, or plans them, look at three figures before the queues start: the energy each fast charger delivers per month, the share of Saturday afternoons it is in use, and how far below its full power it runs when it is. When the third starts to grow, the connection has become the limit, and each of the four answers above takes months, not weeks, to put in place.
