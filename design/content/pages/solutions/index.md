## How the parts work together

On a site with all four, Pilot decides minute by minute where each kilowatt-hour goes: into the building, into the battery, into a vehicle, or out to the grid. This is a working day in May at a distribution centre with a 1.5 MWp roof, a 2 MWh battery and 40 van chargers.

:::list{type="description"}

A day in May

- 00:00 to 05:00
  - The vans charge on cheap night power, slowly enough that the site stays well under its grid limit. The battery fills to 90%.
- 05:00 to 08:00
  - The vans leave full. The cold stores and the conveyors start, and the battery covers the morning peak, so the meter never shows it.
- 08:00 to 16:00
  - The roof produces more than the building uses. The surplus fills the battery, and what it cannot take goes to the grid.
- 16:00 to 20:00
  - Prices peak and the sun goes down. The battery runs the building through the evening, and the vans that come back early start charging from it.
- The month's bill
  - About 40% of the site's electricity came from its own roof, and the highest peak of the month, which sets the grid fee, was 35% lower than before.

:::
