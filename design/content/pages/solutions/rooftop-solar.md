## What you get

A solar system sized to what your building uses in daylight, not to the largest number the roof could hold. Power you use yourself is worth two to three times what the grid pays for a surplus, so we size the system for use on site.

On a flat roof we mount the panels low, facing east and west. That spreads the output over the working day, fits a third more panels on the same roof than rows facing south, and keeps the load on the roof light.

The panels connect to inverters on a wall inside the building, and from there to your main switchboard. Pilot reads every inverter once a minute, so you see what the roof produces and what it saves from the first day.

## Why our customers build solar

:::cards{markers="v"}

### A price fixed for 30 years

Once the system is paid for, its power costs you only the service. Grid prices can rise and fall; the roof's price does not.

### Used where it is made

Power you use on site never passes the grid meter, so you pay no grid fee for it. On a building that runs in daylight, 80% to 95% of the output is used on site.

### Nothing on the ground

The roof does the work. No land to buy or lease, no planning for a field, and the yard stays free for lorries.

### Figures for your annual report

Every kilowatt-hour is measured and logged. Pilot gives you the energy and emission figures your sustainability report asks for, site by site.

## Two ways to pay

The system on the roof is the same either way. What differs is who owns it, and who carries the cost and the risk.

::::tabs

Two ways to pay

### Buy the system

You pay for the system when it is built and own it from the first day. Every kilowatt-hour it produces is yours, free.

Buying gives the lowest cost per kilowatt-hour over the system's life. It suits a company that owns its building, has the capital, and wants the asset on its own balance sheet.

We can run and service the system for you under a separate agreement, priced per kilowatt installed and year.

:::list{type="values"}

- Price, 1 MWp installed
  - €620,000 to €720,000
- Payback
  - 6 to 8 years
- Service agreement
  - €8 to €12 per kWp a year
- Who owns it
  - You, from the first day

:::

### Buy the power

We build, own, insure and run the system on your roof. You pay nothing up front, and buy what it produces at a price per kilowatt-hour fixed for the term of a power purchase agreement.

The price is set below what the same kilowatt-hour costs from the grid today, energy and grid fees together. You pay only for what the roof produces, and our income depends on the system working, so we keep it working.

It suits a company that would rather keep its capital for its own business, or that rents its building. The agreement follows the building if it is sold, with the buyer's consent.

:::list{type="values"}

- Paid up front
  - Nothing
- Term
  - 10 to 20 years
- Price per kWh
  - Fixed, or raised by a set percentage a year
- When the term ends
  - Extend it, buy the system, or have it removed

:::

:::accordion{first="open" expand="one"}

### Will our roof take the weight?

An east–west system adds 12 to 20 kg per m², ballast included, which most industrial roofs built since the 1980s carry with room to spare. We read the building's drawings, and where the margin is unclear a structural engineer calculates it before we make an offer. Where a part of the roof cannot take the load, we leave it empty.

### Does the building have to stop while we build?

No. The installers work on the roof, out of the way of your traffic, and lifts are planned around your deliveries. Connecting the system to your switchboard needs one stop of two to six hours, which we plan at night or at a weekend.

### How long until it pays for itself?

Bought outright, six to nine years, where at least 80% of the output is used on site. The panels last more than 30 years and keep 87% of their output after 30, so the system gives free power for two decades after it has paid for itself. Under a power purchase agreement you save from the first month.

### Who maintains it?

Our own technicians. Pilot reports a fault within minutes, and when a fault stops production a technician is on site within two working days. Every system is inspected once a year, and the panels are cleaned when the yield data shows they need it, usually every two to three years.

### How is it insured?

A system you buy is added to your property insurance; we give your insurer the documents it asks for, and most add it at a small extra premium. Under a power purchase agreement the system is ours, and we insure it.

### What happens when the agreement ends?

You choose. Extend it at a lower price, since the system is paid for by then; buy the system at the price the agreement sets for that year; or have us remove it and restore the roof, at our cost.
