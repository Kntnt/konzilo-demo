## What you get

A battery in one or more containers in your yard, connected to your main switchboard, and controlled by Pilot against your meter, your tariff and the electricity price.

Pilot learns how your site uses power, hour by hour and day by day. When it sees a peak coming it discharges the battery, and in the quiet hours it fills it again, from the grid at night or from your own solar roof by day.

A battery also lets you add machines or chargers on the grid connection you have. Grid operators in all four of our countries have queues for larger connections, and an upgrade can take two years or more. A battery is in place in three to five months.

## Why our customers add a battery

:::cards{markers="v"}

### A lower grid fee every month

Covering the peaks typically lowers the grid fee by 15% to 30%. Kessel Packaging in Dordrecht cut its grid fees by 22% with a 5 MWh battery.

### No wait for a larger connection

The battery covers the hours when the site needs more than the connection gives, so you can grow now rather than after the grid operator's upgrade.

### More of your own solar power used

A battery stores the midday surplus of a solar roof for the evening, instead of selling it to the grid for less.

### Power during a power cut

With added switchgear, the battery keeps chosen loads running, such as cold stores, servers or a process that must not stop.

## Peak shaving alone, or with grid services

A battery can also be paid to help balance the grid. You choose whether it does.

::::tabs

What the battery does

### Peak shaving only

The whole battery is kept for your own site: covering peaks, storing solar power and, with the switchgear for it, backup.

This is the simpler choice, and the right one where the peaks are large and frequent, or where backup matters most. The savings are in your grid bill, and they are easy to follow month by month.

:::list{type="values"}

- Lower grid fee
  - Typically 15% to 30%
- Payback
  - 6 to 9 years
- Battery kept for your site
  - All of it, all year

:::

### With grid services

Part of the battery's capacity is offered to the grid operator, which pays for power held ready to steady the grid's frequency. The battery answers within a second, and is back in the state your site needs within minutes.

Pilot decides each day how much to offer, so that your own peaks are always covered first. What the grid operator pays varies a great deal from year to year, so we show it apart from your savings in the offer, and model it cautiously.

:::list{type="values"}

- Extra income
  - Varies with the market
- Payback
  - 5 to 7 years
- Battery kept for your site
  - Always enough for your forecast peaks

:::

### Where will it stand?

Outdoors in the yard, on a concrete foundation. A 2 MWh battery weighs about 30 tonnes and needs 40 m², with clearances to buildings and roads that we agree with the fire service. It needs no space indoors and no reinforced roof.

### Is a battery a fire risk?

We use lithium iron phosphate cells, the chemistry least prone to overheating. Every container has gas detection, ventilation and fire suppression, and stands apart from the building. The fire service receives the plan and a site visit before the battery is started.

### Does the site have to stop while you connect it?

Only for one planned stop of two to six hours, at night or at a weekend. The foundation and the container are placed without touching your power supply.

### How long until it pays for itself?

Six to nine years for peak shaving alone, five to seven with grid services. The model in your offer shows what the battery would have saved in each of the last 12 months, from your own meter data.

### Who maintains it?

Our own technicians. Pilot watches every cell's temperature and charge once a minute and reports anything unusual at once. We inspect the battery once a year and are on site within two working days of a fault.

### How is it insured?

A battery you buy is added to your property insurance. Insurers ask for the fire plan and the test certificates of the containers, and we give them both. A battery built together with a solar roof can be part of a power purchase agreement, in which we own both, and then we insure it.

### What happens after 15 years?

By then the cells hold about 70% of their first capacity. You can keep using them, replace the cells in the same containers, or have us take the battery away. We send the cells to recycling, at our cost.
