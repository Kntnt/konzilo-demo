## What you get

Chargers in your depot, sized to the distances your vehicles drive and the hours they stand still. We plan them from your routes and your meter data.

Pilot gives each vehicle the power it needs by the time it leaves, and never more than the site's grid connection allows. When the building needs power, the chargers slow down; when a solar roof produces more than the building uses, they speed up.

Drivers plug in and leave. Your fleet manager sees each vehicle's charge, and each charger's use, in Pilot, and the energy per vehicle goes into the monthly report.

## Why our customers charge with us

:::cards{markers="v"}

### No larger grid connection

Load management fits most depots into the connection they have. Where it does not, a battery covers the gap.

### Planned from your routes

We read your routes and standing times, and size each charger to them. Overnight vans need slow chargers; a truck that loads at noon needs a fast one.

### One company for the whole site

The same engineers design the chargers, the solar roof and the battery, and Pilot controls them together.

### Energy per vehicle, every month

Every kilowatt-hour is logged per vehicle and per charger, for your cost accounts and your emission figures.

## What your fleet needs

Vans, trucks and visitors' cars need different chargers. Most of our depots have two kinds.

::::tabs

Kinds of vehicle

### Vans

A van that drives 150 km a day uses about 35 kWh. On an 11 kW charger it is full in three to four hours, and it stands still for ten or more every night.

So vans need slow chargers, one per van, and load management that shares the connection between them. It is the cheapest charging there is, per vehicle and per kilowatt-hour.

:::list{type="values"}

- Charger
  - 11 or 22 kW, alternating current
- Price per charge point, installed
  - €1,800 to €3,000
- Load per 60 vans, managed
  - About 250 kW overnight

:::

### Trucks

A 40-tonne electric truck on regional routes uses 300 to 400 kWh a day. Overnight it needs a charger of 100 to 150 kW; a truck that also tops up while it is loaded at noon needs 350 kW or more for that half hour.

Truck chargers are direct current, and few enough to plan one by one. They are where a battery most often pays: it covers the half hour at noon that the connection cannot.

:::list{type="values"}

- Charger
  - 100 to 400 kW, direct current
- Price per charger, installed
  - €60,000 to €200,000
- Charging time, empty to full
  - 45 minutes to 4 hours

:::

### Visitors and staff

At a retail park or an office, cars stand for one to three hours. Chargers of 11 to 22 kW cover most of them, with a few fast chargers for those in a hurry. Visitors pay by card or app; you set the price.

Westerport Retail Parks has 340 such charge points across eleven parks in the north of England.

:::list{type="values"}

- Charger
  - 11 to 22 kW, and 50 to 150 kW fast chargers
- Payment
  - Card, app or your own staff tags
- Who sets the price
  - You

:::

### Is our grid connection large enough?

Usually, for vans. We read 12 months of your meter data and see how much of the connection is free at night, when most fleets charge. For trucks, or a depot that already runs near its limit, we add a battery or apply for a larger connection, and tell you which costs less.

### Does the depot have to stop while you build?

No. We dig and lay cables in sections, so that part of the yard is always free, and connect the chargers in one planned stop of two to six hours.

### How long until it pays for itself?

The energy for an electric van costs about a quarter of its diesel. For most fleets the depot's chargers are paid back by that difference in two to four years. The vehicles themselves are a separate sum, which your vehicle supplier gives you.

### Who maintains the chargers?

Our own technicians. Pilot sees a charger that stops at once, and in most cases we restart or repair it remotely. When a technician is needed, one is on site within two working days.

### How are the chargers insured?

Chargers you buy go on your property insurance. You can also pay for charging per kilowatt-hour delivered to your vehicles, with nothing up front; the chargers are then ours, and we insure them.

### What happens when the agreement ends?

If you pay per kilowatt-hour, the agreement runs for 8 to 12 years. At its end you extend it, buy the chargers at the price the agreement sets, or have us remove them and restore the ground.
