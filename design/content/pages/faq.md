## <a id="before-you-buy"></a>Before you buy

:::accordion

### Is our roof suitable?

Most flat and low-pitched roofs on industrial buildings are. We look at three things: whether the structure carries 12 to 20 kg per m² more, how many years the roof membrane has left, and how much of the roof is shaded. A membrane with less than ten years left should be replaced first, and we can plan the two together.

### How much of our electricity can solar cover?

For a distribution centre or a factory, typically 20% to 45% of the year's use. The limit is seldom the roof; it is how much power the building uses while the sun shines. We size the system so that at least 80% of its output is used on site.

### What does the site assessment cost, and what do we get?

Nothing. An engineer spends half a day on site, reads 12 months of your meter data, and within two weeks you get a report: the systems that fit, their yield month by month, and their price bought outright and under a power purchase agreement.

### How long from the first visit to power?

Six to nine months for a typical 1 MWp roof. Design and the grid operator's approval take most of it; the building work takes six to twelve weeks.

### Do we need a permit?

Sometimes. Rules for solar on roofs differ between countries and between municipalities, and for a battery the fire service has a say. We find out what applies to your site and make the applications.

:::

## <a id="buying-or-power-purchase"></a>Buying, or a power purchase agreement

:::accordion

### What is a power purchase agreement?

An agreement in which we build, own, insure and run the system on your roof, and you buy the power it produces at a price per kilowatt-hour fixed for 10 to 20 years. You pay nothing up front and nothing for power the system does not produce.

### How is the price per kilowatt-hour set?

From the system's cost, its expected yield over the term, and what the same kilowatt-hour costs you from the grid today, energy and grid fees together. The price is set below that, and either stays fixed or rises by a set percentage a year, as you choose.

### Which costs less over the system's life?

Buying, if you have the capital: you pay no margin for our financing. A power purchase agreement costs more per kilowatt-hour over 20 years, but saves from the first month, keeps your capital for your own business, and leaves the risk of faults and lost yield with us.

### What if we sell or leave the building?

A power purchase agreement follows the building, so a buyer or a new tenant takes it over with the owner's consent. If nobody does, the agreement sets what it costs to end it early, year by year.

### What happens when the agreement ends?

You choose. Extend it at a lower price, buy the system at the price the agreement sets for that year, or have us remove it and restore the roof, at our cost.

:::

## <a id="building"></a>Building

:::accordion

### Do we have to stop work while you build?

No. We work on the roof and in the yard around your operations. Connecting each system to your switchboard needs one planned stop of two to six hours, which we place at night, at a weekend or in your own shutdown.

### Who does the work on site?

Our own installers and electricians, employed by Ampfield, led by a site manager who is your one contact for the whole build. We bring in cranes and, for large roofs, extra roofers, under our supervision.

### Does our roof warranty still hold?

On most flat roofs the panels are held by ballast, so nothing goes through the membrane. Where something must, such as a cable route, we agree it with your roofer or the membrane's maker first, so that their warranty holds.

:::

## <a id="running"></a>Running the system

:::accordion

### Who maintains it?

Our own technicians, from the office nearest your site. Pilot reports faults within minutes, every system is inspected once a year, and when a fault stops production a technician is on site within two working days.

### What if it produces less than you said?

Under a power purchase agreement you pay only for what it produces, so the risk is ours. For a system you buy, our service agreement guarantees 98% availability and pays you for production lost below it.

### What does a northern winter do to the yield?

December and January give 1% to 3% of the year's output in southern Sweden, and a little more in the Netherlands and England. Snow slides off or melts within days, and we design the mounting for the snow load of your area. We do not clear snow; it costs more than it saves.

### Does a solar roof keep the power on in a power cut?

No. The inverters stop when the grid stops, as they must, so that nobody working on the grid is put at risk. Only a battery with the switchgear for it can keep chosen loads running.

:::

## <a id="batteries-and-charging"></a>Batteries and charging

:::accordion

### Is a battery safe beside our building?

We use lithium iron phosphate cells, the chemistry least prone to overheating, in containers with gas detection, ventilation and fire suppression, placed at a distance from the building agreed with the fire service.

### Can a battery earn money as well as save it?

Yes. Part of its capacity can be offered to the grid operator, which pays for power held ready to steady the grid. Pilot makes sure your own peaks are covered first. The income varies from year to year, so we show it apart from your savings.

### Can people outside our company use our chargers?

If you want them to. Chargers can be kept for your own vehicles, opened to staff, or opened to the public with payment by card or app at a price you set.

:::

## <a id="words-we-use"></a>Words we use

:::list{type="description"}

- kWp and MWp
  - Kilowatt peak and megawatt peak: the output of solar panels under standard test conditions, used to state a system's size. 1 MWp is 1,000 kWp.
- kWh and MWh
  - Kilowatt-hour and megawatt-hour: an amount of energy. A 1 MWp roof produces 850 to 1,000 MWh a year in northern Europe.
- Power purchase agreement
  - An agreement to buy the power from a system someone else owns, at a fixed price for a fixed term.
- Peak shaving
  - Using a battery to cover the short peaks in a site's use, so that the grid connection never sees them.
- Use on site
  - The share of a solar system's output used in the building, rather than sold to the grid. Also called self-consumption.
- Grid connection
  - The most power a site may draw from the grid at once, agreed with the grid operator.

:::
