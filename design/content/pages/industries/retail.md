## What matters in retail property

The landlord pays for the power of the common parts: car park lighting, signs, heating and cooling of malls, lifts and escalators. Most of it is used in opening hours, which is when a roof produces, so a modest system covers much of it.

Tenants increasingly ask for the emissions of the space they rent, and some leases now require the landlord to cut them. Pilot reports energy and emissions per park and per unit, in a form a tenant can put in its own report.

Visitors with electric cars choose where to shop by where they can charge. Chargers of 11 to 22 kW suit a visit of an hour or two, and a few fast chargers serve those passing through. You set the price, and the chargers can pay for themselves.
